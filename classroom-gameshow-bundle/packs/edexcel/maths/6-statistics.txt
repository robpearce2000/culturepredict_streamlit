# S1 Samples and populations

Q: What word describes a sample whose members reflect the make-up of the whole population?
A: Representative
Accept: Representative sample; Unbiased
Ref: S1
D: 1

Q: What name is given to a survey that collects data from every member of a population?
A: Census
Accept: A census
Ref: S1
D: 1

Q: In a sample of 25 pupils, 4 own a rabbit. Estimate how many of the school's 500 pupils own a rabbit.
A: 80
Accept: 80 pupils
Ref: S1
D: 2

Q: In a sample of 60 cars, 12 are red. Estimate the fraction of all cars that are red, in its simplest form.
A: 1/5
Accept: One fifth; 0.2; 20%
Ref: S1
D: 2

Q: In a sample of 30 apples, 3 are bruised. Estimate how many apples are bruised in a crate of 450.
A: 45
Accept: 45 apples
Ref: S1
D: 2
Note: 3 out of 30 is one tenth, and one tenth of 450 is 45.

Q: Why is asking only 5 people unlikely to give a good estimate for a whole town?
A: Sample is too small
Accept: Too small a sample; Sample size too small; Not enough people asked
Ref: S1
D: 3

Q: A sample of 50 voters gives 30% for a party; a sample of 500 voters gives 26%. Which estimate is more reliable?
A: 26%
Accept: 26; The sample of 500; The larger sample
Ref: S1
D: 3
Note: The larger sample gives the more reliable estimate.
